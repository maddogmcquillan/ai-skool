# Ad swipe file: the lines picked for the ads, organized by avatar and angle

Every line below is verbatim from `docs/RESEARCH-AVATARS.md`, chosen by the owner as material to
build ads from. Nothing is reworded; only the grouping is new.

The overarching angle, which every ad is a specific case of: kids are already using AI, and nobody
is teaching them how. Teachers are not, and parents do not know how to. Each ad picks one age
bracket and one specific reason they have not learned it, and the landing page carries the rest.

## 0. Headline-ready numbers (use with any avatar)

Kids already use it:

- "86% of kids aged 9 to 17 already use AI. One in four uses it every day."
- "9 in 10 teens use AI." "Two-thirds of teens use AI chatbots."

They do not understand it:

- "Only 1 in 5 kids say they know a lot about how AI works."
- Kids 9-17 who say they know "a lot" about how AI works / "a little" / nothing: 21% / 68% / 12%

Nobody at school is teaching it:

- "Over 80% of students say no teacher has explicitly taught them how to use AI."
- "More than 8 in 10 students say no teacher has taught them how to use AI
- Students who say no teacher has explicitly taught them how to use AI for schoolwork: more than 80%

## How the avatars split

> parents of 11- to 13-year-olds are buying safety and a better use of screen time; parents of
> 14- to 17-year-olds are buying preparation and a path after school. The reviews (Part 4) add a
> third cut that is not about age at all: the buy trigger is almost always the kid, already
> obsessed with making something and stuck, and the parent who has usually tried and been burned
> by something else.

## Avatar 1. Parents of 11- to 13-year-olds (middle school)

What they are buying: safety and a better use of screen time.

### Angle 1A. He is already using AI and I can't explain it.

> He is already using AI and I can't explain it. 64% of teens use chatbots, 51% of parents think
> theirs does. Only 1 in 5 kids say they know a lot about how AI works. "'Mom, what is AI?
> Everyone in class is talking about it.' I tried to explain from what I knew." "I realized I had
> no idea how to actually explain it to him." "They are growing up bilingual in AI without anyone
> teaching them." "I have zero visibility into what it's telling her."

- "My son came home talking about ai and I realized I had no idea how to actually explain it to
  him in a way that made sense." (r/raisingkids, Mar 2026)
- "Parents talking to children may be the 'blind leading the blind' in most households."
  (Substack comment, Feb 2025)
- "I feel like we're the first generation of parents dealing with this and there's no playbook."
  (r/daddit, Aug 2025, 190 comments)

### Angle 1B. Screen time that builds something

- Screen-time guilt (46% of teens online almost constantly; 4 in 10 families argue weekly).
- Reply: "My 12 yo loves video games... He got introduced to Microsoft MakeCode and now he codes
  games and gives them to his friends to play. This I find much more productive, and don't limit
  his time doing this as much."

### Angle 1C. The shortcut fear, and nothing in between

> "a kid using AI as a substitute for thinking"; "accidentally outsourcing the 'productive
> struggle' that actually builds a brain"; "Everything I find online is either 'ban it completely'
> or 'let them use ChatGPT for homework' — nothing in between that actually teaches kids how AI
> works." 83% of parents want kids who can think without AI; 68% also want them to learn to use
> it well.

- "Been homeschooling for 5 years and honestly AI is the one subject I have zero idea how to
  approach. Everything I find online is either 'ban it completely' or 'let them use ChatGPT for
  homework' — nothing in between that actually teaches kids how AI works or how to think
  critically about it... Feels like a gap nobody's really solved yet." (r/homeschool, Sept 2026)
- "It feels like we missed a window where AI literacy should have been part of how she learns,
  not the first thing she runs to when thinking gets hard." (parent who works in tech, Jul 2026)
- They are growing up bilingual in AI without anyone teaching them." ... "she stopped writing
  first drafts on her own for a while." House rules: "No AI before she has her own first thought
  on paper... Show me what YOU asked it, not just what it gave you." (parent who builds AI
  systems, 13-year-old, May 2026)
- Teens who say students at their school use AI chatbots to cheat at least somewhat often: 59%
  (76% among teens who use chatbots for schoolwork)

## Avatar 2. Parents of 14- to 17-year-olds (high school)

What they are buying: preparation and a path after school.

### Angle 2A. School teaches the rules, not the skill.

> School teaches the rules, not the skill. 74% of students now report school AI rules; 28% are
> given any tool; more than 8 in 10 say no teacher has taught them to use it. "I haven't heard of
> a single high school teaching seniors what AI means for their future. No literacy, no warnings,
> no prep. Just 'work hard and climb the ladder'

- "I haven't heard of a single high school teaching seniors what AI means for their future. No
  literacy, no warnings, no prep. Just 'work hard and climb the ladder' — but half the rungs are
  already gone." (r/Parenting, Sept 2025)
- "Only 1 in 10 students say their teachers have helped prepare them to use AI in future jobs or
  education."
- "77% of parents want schools to teach kids to use AI tools effectively. 85% say AI skills matter
  for their child's career."
- Parents who want responsible AI taught across all classes: 58%

### Angle 2B. The job market has already moved

- "71% of leaders would rather hire a less experienced candidate with AI skills than a more
  experienced one without."
- "What jobs will be left for people? How do you raise kids for an AI world?" (Mumsnet, 2023)
- "By the time my kids finish school, artificial intelligence will have fundamentally reshaped
  the world they'll enter as adults, and I worry about that a lot." (Sasha Fegan, Center for
  Humane Technology, Apr 2025)
- "Is anyone else freaking out about AI & kids?" (Thoughts From A Digital Mom, Jun 2025)

### Angle 2C. He uses it, but can't explain it or show anything.

> He uses it, but can't explain it or show anything. "I don't want him to just become someone who
> knows how to type prompts. I'd rather he actually understands how AI works, how to use it
> responsibly, and how to build things with it" (parent of a 13-year-old).

- "The 'people not using AI get left behind, people overly reliant on AI get left behind' framing
  is exactly where my wife and I keep landing too. The middle path is the work." (May 2026)

## Avatar 3. The kid who is already building (any age)

What triggers the buy: the kid, already obsessed with making something and stuck, and a parent
who has usually tried something else first.

- "I can't just tell my nephew to go talk to chatgpt all day long and learn, without a project
  plan/target, he's gonna drift off." (Hacker News, Aug 2025)
- "They focus on building cool AI projects instead of just prompt writing, so he's learning how
  AI actually works." (parent of a 12-year-old, Jul 2026)
- "watching a video series about using AI is pretty different from having a real project with a
  person checking your work and making you defend your choices. That's the part that seems to
  actually change how a 13 or 16 year old thinks." (Sept 2026)
- Now my 13 year old is working with some friends to develop a game." (Jan 2026)

## How to end any ad: the proud moment

- "came home excited to share what he had built and discovered"
- "After every session Brayden was proud to show me what he had learned."
- "It was fun, nice to see her imagination, and then her joy when she was able to see her idea on
  paper." (r/daddit, "Daughter designs her own coloring book with ChatGPT", May 2025, 761 upvotes)

## The words parents use for the outcome they want

- understand how AI actually works / not just type prompts / know when the output is junk
- build things with it / creating, not consuming / something productive
- a tool, not a crutch
- think for themselves / use their actual brain / critical thinking
