# Who buys Learn AI, and why: avatars, pain points and angles for ads

Research for the ad creative. Everything below is sourced; verbatim parent language is quoted, not
paraphrased. Updated 2026-10-02.

## Part 1. The evidence base (primary sources only)

### 1.1 Kids already use AI, far more than parents know

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tweens 9-12 who use or interact with AI | 81% (17% daily) | Common Sense Media, *AI Use by Tweens and Teens* census, 2026 |
| Teens 13-15 / 16-17 who use AI | 89% (28% daily) / 92% (30% daily) | same |
| 9- to 12-year-olds who have used a chatbot (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude...) | 58% | same |
| Kids 9-17 who say they use AI **to create things** (images, video, stories, music, apps) | 75%, including 74% of 9- to 12-year-olds | same |
| Kids who use AI for homework help at least weekly / daily | 48% / 21% | same |
| Daily users who say a month without AI would be hard | 42% | same |
| US teens 13-17 who use AI chatbots | 64%, about 3 in 10 daily | Pew Research Center, *How Teens Use and View AI*, Feb 24 2026 (n=1,458 teens and parents, Sept-Oct 2025) |
| Teens who use AI for schoolwork | 54% (10% for most or all of it) | same |
| Parents who say their teen uses chatbots / are not sure | 51% / 30% | same |
| Teens who used ChatGPT for schoolwork, 2023 vs 2024 | 13% to 26%; 31% of 11th-12th graders | Pew, Jan 15 2025 |
| Parents aware their teen uses generative AI (2024) | 37% | Common Sense Media, *The Dawn of the AI Era*, Sept 2024 |

Reading: the "should my kid use AI?" question is over for this age group. They already do, daily, on
their own devices, and three quarters already *make* things with it. The gap is between using and
understanding, and between dabbling and building something finished.

### 1.2 Parents' fear is about jobs and preparation, and it is near-universal

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Parents of under-18s who think AI will make it **harder** for their child to find a job (vs easier) | 57% vs 29% | Common Sense Media, *Generation AI*, Mar 2026 (parents n=843, kids 12-17 n=800, Nov 2025) |
| Parents who agree kids should prepare for the future by **learning how best to use AI tools** | 68% (kids: 77%) | same |
| Parents who agree kids must learn to think critically **without** AI | 83% | same |
| Parents who think people will be so dependent on AI they cannot function without it | 71% | same |
| Parents who worry AI will undermine creativity | 70% | same |
| Parents of 12- to 24-year-olds who fear AI could disrupt or replace their child's career within a decade | 97% | Zety, *AI Readiness Gap: Parent Outlook*, Oct 2025 |
| Parents who say schools are not equipping students with AI-era skills | 36% | same |
| Parents who see technical skills (coding, AI, data) as the most critical for their child | 52% | same |
| Parents heavily involved, or planning to be, in guiding their child's career path | 71% | same |
| Parents of Gen Z anxious about whether their child is prepared for AI-era jobs / unsure how to feel | 55% / 33% | Penn Foster survey, 2025 |
| Parents who believe AI will open career opportunities that do not exist yet | 75% | same |
| Parents who view AI skills as important for their child | 67% | Learner.com survey, Mar 2026 (n=420) |
| Parents who want to learn more about AI themselves | 85% | same |
| Young workers 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations: employment vs. where it would otherwise be | 19% below, via reduced hiring of young workers, not layoffs | Stanford Digital Economy Lab, *Canaries in the Coal Mine*, Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen, rev. Aug 2026 |
| Jobs the WEF expects displaced vs created by 2030 | 92 million displaced, 170 million created | World Economic Forum, *Future of Jobs Report 2025* |

Reading: fear of the job market is the most widely shared emotion, but parents do not want AI *instead
of* thinking. The winning promise is "understands it and can build with it", not "uses it more".

### 1.3 School is not where this gets learned, and parents know it

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Students who say their school has **no clear AI policy** | 49% | Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, *Voices of Gen Z: How American Youth View and Use AI*, Apr 2025 (n=3,465, incl. 1,460 aged 13-18) |
| Middle schools / high schools with stated AI rules | 45% / 54% | same |
| Schools with policies that allow vs ban AI | roughly even | same |
| Students in counties with median income $95k+ whose schools have AI policies | 66% | same |
| Students in counties under $55k median income: school bans AI vs allows | more likely banned (19%) than allowed | same |
| Students who say schools should be **required** to help them understand how to leverage AI / who want to be allowed to use it for classwork | 52% / 47% | same |
| Teens who say students at their school use AI chatbots to cheat at least somewhat often | 59% (76% among teens who use chatbots for schoolwork) | Pew, Feb 2026 |
| Kids whose school has told them what AI may and may not be used for in schoolwork | 73% | Common Sense census, 2026 |
| Gen Z who feel anxious about AI | 41% | same |
| Teens who say school has no AI rules or they are unsure | 60% | Common Sense Media, 2024 |
| Parents who say schools have not communicated about generative AI | 83% | same |
| Kids whose school has discussed how to use AI safely / has not | 56% / 44% | Common Sense census, 2026 |
| Kids who have heard from school how to tell if AI information is accurate | 51% | same |
| Teachers with no training on generative AI (winter 2026) | 42% | EdWeek Research Center |
| Districts without a districtwide AI initiative | 73% | EdWeek Research Center, Sept-Nov 2025 |
| Parents who want responsible AI taught across all classes | 58% | Child Trends, Dec 2025 (n=720 parents of 12- to 18-year-olds) |
| Parents unaware of their school's AI policy | 50% | Learner.com, Mar 2026 |

Caveat for copy: an EdWeek survey of 499 *educators* (Dec-Jan) found about 8 in 10 say high
schoolers get lessons on "what AI is and how to use it responsibly". So the defensible line is not
"schools teach nothing". It is: schools teach the rules, and half the kids can't even tell you what
those rules are. Nobody at school is teaching them to **build** with it. "Kids don't learn this in
school" holds for *this* ("this" = making things with AI); "schools ignore AI" does not.

### 1.4 Screen time is the guilt underneath everything

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Teens online "almost constantly" | 46% | Pew, *Teens and Screens*, Mar 2024 (n=1,453 teen-parent pairs) |
| Parents and teens who argue regularly about phone time | 4 in 10 each | same |
| Teens who say they spend too much time on their phone | 38% | same |
| Teens on at least one of the big five platforms almost constantly | 36% | Pew, *Teens, Social Media and AI Chatbots*, Dec 2025 |
| Teens who use TikTok daily | 61% | same |

Reading: a parent will not say "I want to buy my kid more screen time". They will pay to convert
screen time they already lose the argument about into something they can be proud of.

### 3.3 What nobody is saying (open lanes for Learn AI)

1. **Named, Google-able instructors.** Every competitor hides behind "Stanford alumni" or "top 1% globally". Nobody names a teacher a parent can look up in ten seconds. Our roster (Kevin Stratvert, Jeff Su, Tina Huang, 3Blue1Brown, IBM Technology, Tech With Tim) is proof no one else can claim, and it answers the "not a pre-recorded course" attack: the recordings are the point, because they are the best ones.
2. **Help between classes.** Self-paced apps leave the kid alone; live schools help for the booked hour; cohorts cost $945+. "A coach that answers in minutes, any night" is uncontested.
3. **"Already using it, now understand it."** The surveys show the worry; only school-facing products touch it, and only as anti-cheating.
4. **A finished-things portfolio for teens under $100/month.** Kubrio does it at 6-13 for $99. Nobody at 11-17.
5. **Community design as the safety promise.** No member messaging, an adult reads every thread. Competitors' safety is chat monitoring or "live-monitored" classes.
6. **The teen screen-time reframe.** "Same laptop, a different kind of hour." Open above 13.

### 3.4 What the strongest funnels do that we do not

- **Every paid competitor removes first-purchase risk** with a named mechanism: 7-day free trial (Synthesis, Kodable), 14 days free (CodaKid), 30-day money back (Tynker), four-session money back (CodeWizardsHQ), free trial class (every live school), 7-day refund (Kubrio). Learn AI is the only paid product in the set with neither a trial nor a guarantee. The 7-day trial decision matters more than any headline.
- **Price-first creative with anchors.** Synthesis's top formats are "Offer-First Banner" and strikethrough pricing. $49 reframed is "about $1.60 a day" or "less than two Outschool sessions".
- **Show the kid's output, not the curriculum.** Quest leads with named kids' projects; AI Camp with "310+ Unique AI products built by students"; iD Tech with the hardware you take home. Our project grid is the same move; real kid-made artifacts would finish it.
- **Specific odd-number proof.** "Less than 5% of teacher candidates are selected"; "251 students enrolled in last 7 days"; "11,000+ parents joined in the last week". Our spots-taken bar is the start of this.
- **UGC parent-voice ads, no hard CTA, 8 new creatives a week** (Synthesis, Outschool). Volume of creative over one hero ad.
